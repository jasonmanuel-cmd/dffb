# Financial Comparison: Sell vs Partner vs Hold Vacant

Simple scenarios to use in outreach and pitch conversations.

## Scenario 1: Single-Family House (3 beds, valued at $400,000)

### Option A: Sell to Flipper / Cash Buyer
- Sale price: $300,000 (20% discount for quick cash)
- Closing costs: $15,000
- **Net to owner: $285,000 (one-time)**

### Option B: Partner with DFFB (Revenue Share)
- Property value: retain ownership at ~$400,000
- DFFB converts to 3–4 bed senior home
- Monthly revenue: $8,000 (occupancy + care services)
- DFFB take: 30%, Landlord take: 70% = $5,600/month
- First year net: $67,200
- Ongoing: $67,200/year (passive income)
- 5-year cumulative: $336,000 + retained property asset worth $400,000

### Option C: Sell to DFFB (Full Exit)
- Fair market value: $350,000–$380,000
- Closing costs: $17,500
- **Net to owner: $332,500–$362,500 (one-time, clean exit)**

### Option D: Hold Vacant (Do Nothing)
- Annual costs: property tax $4,000, insurance $1,200, maintenance/vacancy $2,000 = $7,200/year
- Property appreciation (modest): +2%/year = $8,000/year
- 5-year net: -$36,000 + $40,000 appreciation = $4,000 (+ ongoing liability & stress)

### Comparison at Year 5

| Option | Year 1 Income | Year 5 Cumulative | Asset Retained | Comment |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| A: Sell (Flipper) | $285k | $285k | None | Liquidity now; no ongoing upside |
| B: Partner (Revenue) | $67.2k | $336k + $400k asset | Yes ($400k) | Passive income + growth; long-term play |
| C: Sell to DFFB | $347.5k | $347.5k | None | Clean exit; capital available for reinvestment |
| D: Hold Vacant | -$7.2k | +$4k (net) | Yes ($408k) | Most expensive option; no income, tax/insurance drain |

## Scenario 2: Multi-Bed House (6 beds, valued at $600,000)

### Option A: Sell to Flipper
- Sale: $420,000 (30% discount for quick cash)
- Closing: $21,000
- **Net: $399,000**

### Option B: Partner with DFFB (Revenue Share)
- Monthly revenue: $12,000 (6 residents × ~$2,000/resident)
- DFFB take: 25%, Landlord take: 75% = $9,000/month
- Year 1 net: $108,000
- 5-year cumulative: $540,000 + property (now worth $650k+ if well-operated)

### Option C: Sell to DFFB
- Fair market: $520,000–$560,000
- Closing: $26,000–$28,000
- **Net: $492,000–$532,000**

### Comparison at Year 5

| Option | Year 1 Income | Year 5 Cumulative | Asset Retained | Comment |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| A: Sell (Flipper) | $399k | $399k | None | Quick liquidity; no upside |
| B: Partner (Revenue) | $108k | $540k + $650k asset | Yes ($650k) | Strong passive income + appreciation |
| C: Sell to DFFB | $511k | $511k | None | Clean exit; capital available |

## Key Talking Points

1. **Passive Income vs. Fire Sale**
   - "Partnering typically returns 2–3x more over 5 years than selling to a cash buyer."

2. **Retained Asset & Appreciation**
   - "Keep the property, earn monthly income, and benefit from property appreciation as the business scales."

3. **Liquidity Option**
   - "If you need cash now, we can buy it outright at a fair price. You're not forced to partner."

4. **Opportunity Cost of Vacancy**
   - "Holding it vacant costs you money every month and generates zero income. This property is your customer."

5. **Simplicity**
   - "You get a monthly check. We handle residents, staffing, licensing, and compliance. No landlord headaches."

## How to Customize These Numbers

- Use actual local property values and rental/care rates in your target market
- Adjust DFFB's percentage share based on the deal structure
- Include realistic occupancy ramp (70% Year 1, 85% Year 2, 95% Year 3)
- Add modest property appreciation (2–3%/year) and inflation
- Include operating cost trends (staffing, utilities) if detailed P&L is useful

Use these examples in:
- Initial property conversation (show why partnering makes sense)
- Pitch deck for heirs/owners
- Follow-up email with a visual or simple table
