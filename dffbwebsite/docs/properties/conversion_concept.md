# Property Conversion Concept

What the property becomes under DFFB's model + operational framework.

---

## The Conversion Concept

### From → To

**Single-Family Home or Multi-Bed House**
- From: vacant, distressed, or underutilized residential property
- To: licensed, professionally-managed senior residential care community (6–12 beds typical)

**Small Commercial Property**
- From: empty office, retail, or light industrial space
- To: specialized care home with activity spaces, dining, common areas

---

## Operational Model (Generic)

### Resident Population
- Independent to assisted-living seniors (55+, typically)
- Focus: aging in place, social connection, light daily assistance
- Typical stay: 2–7 years (varies by resident acuity)

### Staffing
- Manager (full-time): oversees operations, licensing, compliance, staff
- Licensed caregivers (varies by state): hands-on resident care, ADL assistance
- Activity/social coordinator: engagement, outings, events
- Chef/cook or meal service: meal prep, dietary requirements
- Housekeeping/maintenance (full or part-time)
- Typical ratios: 1 staff : 2–3 residents during day; lower at night

### Resident Services
- 24/7 supervision and care availability
- Medications management
- Meal preparation and dining
- Housekeeping, laundry
- Activity programming (outings, socializing, fitness)
- Coordination with family, medical providers, and specialists

### Licensing & Compliance
- RCFE (Residential Care Facility for the Elderly) license (state-specific; varies by care level)
- Health & safety code compliance (fire, ADA, plumbing, electrical, etc.)
- Staff background checks and certifications
- Regular inspections and reporting
- Insurance: liability, workers' comp, property

---

## Revenue Model

### Income Streams
1. **Resident fees** (primary): $1,500–$3,500/resident/month depending on care level and region
2. **Additional services**: incontinence supplies, transportation, medication management add-ons (+ $200–$500/resident/month)
3. **Optional activities**: concierge services, in-home salon, fitness classes (+ $100–$300/resident/month)

### Cost Structure (Simplified)
- **Staffing:** 60–70% of revenue (largest cost)
- **Operating:** utilities, food, supplies, maintenance: 15–20%
- **Licensing & compliance:** insurance, inspections, training: 5–8%
- **Owner/landlord share:** 10–20% (if partnership model)

### Typical Margin
- Well-run facility at 90% occupancy: 15–25% net margin

## Conversion Timeline

### Pre-Conversion (0–2 months)
- Property assessment and zoning review
- Licensing research and application prep
- Capital plan: budget renovations and furnishings
- Staffing plan: identify roles, recruitment timeline

### Conversion Phase 1: Physical (1–4 months)
- Renovations: bathroom accessibility, kitchen upgrade, common areas
- Safety compliance: fire suppression, alarms, emergency systems
- ADA modifications: ramps, grab bars, door widths
- Furnishing: beds, dining furniture, activity spaces

### Conversion Phase 2: Licensing & Staffing (parallel)
- Submit license application
- Hire and train staff
- Develop policies and procedures
- Set up resident intake and admission process

### Phase 3: Soft Launch (1–2 months)
- Accept first residents (often slower initial ramp)
- Stabilize operations, train staff in real-world situations
- Address issues quickly

### Phase 4: Stabilize & Optimize (ongoing)
- Ramp to full occupancy (typically 6–12 months)
- Refine operations, marketing, and referral networks
- Build community partnerships

**Total timeline to profitability:** typically 6–12 months

## Why This Works

### For the Property Owner
- Leverages idle asset
- Generates reliable monthly income (or full exit)
- Professional management removes landlord headaches
- Property value often increases under good management

### For DFFB
- Addresses real senior housing shortage
- Recurring revenue model (residents stay 2–7 years average)
- Scalable: replicate model across properties
- High barrier to entry for competitors (licensing, staffing)

### For Residents
- Home-like environment vs. large institutional facility
- Personalized care and attention
- Community and social connection
- Aging in place in familiar neighborhood

## Risk Mitigations

### Operational Risks
- Licensed, trained staff
- Proper insurance coverage
- Regular compliance audits
- Family communication protocols
- Emergency response plans

### Financial Risks
- Conservative occupancy assumptions (80% target)
- Diversified resident base (don't rely on one payer source)
- Reserve fund for vacancies and repairs
- Clear lease/agreement terms

### Market Risks
- Senior population growing (demographic tailwind)
- Shortage of quality small-scale senior housing (demand advantage)
- Multiple exit options if need to pivot

---

## Success Metrics

**Operational:**
- Occupancy: target 85–95% within 12 months
- Resident retention: target 90%+ year-over-year
- Staff retention: target 80%+ annually
- Compliance: zero violations or fines

**Financial:**
- Revenue per occupied bed: $2,000–$3,000/month
- Net margin: 15–25% at steady state
- Payback period: 3–5 years for conversion investment

**Community:**
- Resident satisfaction: 90%+ positive feedback
- Local referral partnerships: 10+ active
- Staff NPS (Net Promoter Score): 70+
