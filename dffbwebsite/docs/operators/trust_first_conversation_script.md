Trust-First Conversation Script
------------------------------

Purpose: open a low-pressure conversation that identifies pain, offers relief, and sets a tiny next step.

1) Opening (30s)
"Hi [Name], I appreciate you taking a minute. I’m [Your] with DFFB — we help RCFE operators reduce operational stress and improve occupancy while keeping staff and resident continuity. I’m not here to replace you; I just want to learn whether you have any pressures we could help with. Is now a bad time?"

2) Two quick questions (ask and listen)
- "How are you doing on occupancy right now?"
- "Any pressing staffing or regulatory headaches this month?"

3) Empathy + micro-help (30–60s)
- Reflect back: "That sounds stressful — short staffing makes everything harder."
- Offer immediate, low-effort help: "We have a short checklist and a placement channel that sometimes helps fill one or two beds quickly. If you’d like, I can send it or schedule 10 minutes to walk through it. No commitment."

4) If interested → narrow next step
- "Great — would you rather I email the checklist now or hop on a 10-minute call tomorrow at [two options]?"

5) If hesitant → leave the door open
- "I get it. If anything changes, may I send occasional helpful updates about staffing and simple occupancy tactics? You can unsubscribe anytime."

6) Close
- Confirm next step, thank them, and follow up immediately with promised material.

Notes:
- Keep the tone not transactional: aim for problem-first language, not DFFB-first.
- Never lead with acquisition or takeover language; use "support," "stabilize," "legacy preservation."
